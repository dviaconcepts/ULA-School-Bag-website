Uganda Lasting School Bag · Funded by the Government of Uganda

One good bag for every Ugandan child.

ULA is a national campaign to design and make a strong leather school bag that families can afford, so that every young Ugandan can carry their books to school with pride, year after year.

The campaign

A school bag built for Ugandan roads, rain and years of use.

Too many children carry their books in thin bags that tear within a term. ULA brings government funding, local design talent and local leather together to make one bag that lasts, at a price every family can manage. The bag is being designed, tested and made in Uganda by SASA College of Industrial Design.

Promise 01 · Lasting

Made to last

Real leather and reinforced stitching, designed to survive long walks, rainy seasons and being handed down to a younger sibling.

Promise 02 · Affordable

Priced for families

Public funding covers the design and development work, so the finished bag can reach parents at a fair, affordable price.

Promise 03 · For all

For every child

Designed for pupils across Uganda, in town and village, from the first year of primary school onward.

Why leather

Uganda raises the hides. Ugandan hands should turn them into something our children use every day.

Strength
Leather resists tearing and scuffing far longer than cheap synthetic fabric, so one bag can serve for several school years.
Repairable
A torn seam or loose strap can be fixed by any local cobbler or leather worker, instead of the bag being thrown away.
Local value
Using Ugandan leather keeps more of the value chain at home, supporting tanners, artisans and makers.
Design
Shaped by a market study and user preference testing, so the bag fits how Ugandan pupils really carry their load.
Skills
Local artisans are trained in leather cutting and production, with know-how drawn from benchmarking visits to China, Kenya and Ghana.

Our journey

Four milestones, one lasting bag.

ULA began in June 2026 and is moving step by step, from first sketches to bags on sale for families. Each milestone is done properly before the next begins.

Product development

Designing the bag around what pupils and parents need.

  • Market study
  • Prototype design & sample materials
  • User preference testing
  • Artisan training
Done
M2Oct 2026 – now

Workshop setup

Building a workshop that can make bags at scale.

  • Furniture & fittings
  • Benchmarking in China, Kenya & Ghana
  • Equipment, machines & tools
  • Further artisan training
In progress

Making the first 100 ULA bags.

  • Raw materials & accessories
  • Packaging & licensing
